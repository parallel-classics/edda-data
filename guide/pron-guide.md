# How to say the names

Mímir shows how an Icelander says each name **today**. Icelandic is the living descendant of Old Norse, and its spelling has barely changed. Where the modern form differs from the Old Norse headword, the card gives it: Þórr is now Þór, Baldr is Baldur.

**Stress always falls on the first syllable**: ÓÐ-inn, never Ó-ÐINN. In compounds, each later part takes a lighter stress (Ás-garður, Völu-spá). Respellings put the stressed syllable in CAPITALS.

## The letters that matter

| Letter | Sounds like | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| þ | **th** in *thin* | Þórr THOHR, Þjálfi THYOWL-vi |
| ð | **dh**: the *th* in *this* | Óðinn OH-dhin, Iðunn I-dhun |
| æ | **eye** (*igh* after a consonant) | Æsir EYE-sir, Hænir HIGH-nir |
| á | **ow** in *cow* | Ásgarðr OWS-gar-dhur, Fáfnir FOWP-nir |
| ó | **oh** | Óðinn OH-dhin, Sól SOHL |
| ö | **ur** in *fur*, with no r sound (an extra r is written *urr*) | Völuspá VUR-lu-spow, Njörðr NYURR-dhur |
| au | **oy**, but with rounded lips, like French *œil* | Draupnir DROYP-nir, Laufey LOYV-ay |
| ei, ey | **ay** in *day* | Freyja FRAY-ya, Heimdallr HAYM-dat-lur |
| ll | **tl** | Valhöll VAHL-hurtl, Yggdrasill IG-dra-sitl |
| hv | **kv** | Hvergelmir KVEHR-gel-mir, Hveðrungr KVEDH-roong-gur |
| fn, fl | **pn**, **pl** | Fáfnir FOWP-nir, Niflheimr NIPL-hay-mur |
| rn, nn | **rtn**; nn is **tn** after an accented vowel or *ei* | nornir NORT-nir, Frekasteinn FREH-ka-staytn |
| r | always sounded, lightly rolled, even at the end | Freyr FRAYR, Týr TEER |
| j | **y** in *yes* | jötnar YURT-nar, Gjúki GYOO-ki |

Respelling key: **ee** as in *see* (í, ý), **oo** as in *moon* (ú), **aw** as in *law* (long o), **ah** as in *father* (long a), **eh** as in *bed*, held longer (long e), **yeh** for é. Plain **u** is the short Icelandic u, between *put* and French *tu*. **g** is always hard, as in *go*; **y** is the sound of *yes*. **kh** is the *ch* of Scottish *loch*.

A few more habits: **sl** sounds *stl* (Sleipnir STLAYP-nir); **g** before *i* or *j* after a vowel becomes **y** (Ægir EYE-yir, Sigyn SEE-yin); *a*, *e* and *o* before *gi* turn into diphthongs (Bragi BRIGH-yi, Logi LOY-yi). Icelanders breathe a little **h** before *pp, tt, kk* and before *p, t, k* followed by *l* or *n* (Sleipnir, Grótti, Brokkr). The respellings leave this out, but the IPA shows it as [ʰ].

## Medieval and modern

Snorri, writing in the 1220s, sounded different. Scholars reconstruct long vowels where Icelandic now has diphthongs: *á* was "aa", *ó* "oo", *æ* a long open "e", and *au* "ow". *Ll* and *rn* were plain l and rn. Mímir gives only the modern pronunciation, the one Icelanders use when they read the Eddas aloud.
